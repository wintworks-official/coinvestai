1. The K-Score Machine Learning Engine — Explained

The K-Score is Kavout's core intellectual property — a machine learning-derived numerical rating from 1 to 9 assigned daily to thousands of US-listed equities. Think of it as a quantitative analyst's multi-factor model condensed into a single number that every investor can understand at a glance:

📊 K-Score Reference Scale — Investment Signal Interpretation
1
Strong Sell Signal
2
High Underperform Risk
3
Underperform Likely
4
Slight Underperform
5
Market Neutral
6
Slight Outperform
7
Moderate Outperform
8
Strong Outperform
9
Highest Conviction Buy

A K-Score of 9 indicates the highest statistical probability of outperforming the S&P 500 over a forward-looking 1-to-3 month horizon. A score of 1 signals probable underperformance. The model updates daily, dynamically adjusting scores as new fundamental data, price action, and market sentiment inputs are processed.

The K-Score is generated by deep neural networks and gradient boosting decision trees (GBDT) synthesizing four multi-factor analytical dimensions:

📊

Fundamental Quality & Value

Evaluates corporate balance sheet health, cash flow sustainability, return on equity (ROE), earnings quality, and historical valuation multiples relative to industry peer benchmarks.

📈

Technical Momentum & Trend

Identifies multi-timeframe price breakouts, volume anomalies, moving average divergences, relative strength versus sector benchmarks, and momentum persistence signals.

⚡

Volatility & Downside Risk

Quantifies Beta sensitivity, historical maximum drawdown probability, realized volatility distributions, and statistical variance to prevent high-risk value traps from entering high-score buckets.

🌊

Market Sentiment & Money Flow

Monitors institutional accumulation patterns, options flow telemetry, earnings transcript sentiment shifts extracted via NLP, and retail sentiment divergence indicators.

📌 Why Multi-Factor Synthesis Matters: Each individual factor — fundamentals, momentum, sentiment — has historically worked well in specific market environments but failed in others. Kavout's machine learning engine dynamically weights these factors based on regime conditions, providing more robust signals than any single-factor approach.

3. Investment & Trading Applications

Kavout is not a one-size-fits-all tool. Understanding which investment strategies align with its K-Score methodology is critical for extracting maximum value from the platform:

Where Kavout Excels: Optimal Investment Applications

  • Swing trading candidate identification: Filter for K-Score 8-9 stocks within a preferred sector to build a watchlist of statistically favored setups for 2-6 week holding periods
  • Portfolio quarterly rebalancing: Use monthly K-Score updates to identify positions that have deteriorated in multi-factor quality, enabling systematic position trimming
  • Factor-based ETF construction: Build thematic baskets (e.g., top-25 K-Score technology stocks) with systematic weekly rebalancing as a rules-based investment approach
  • Long/short pair construction: Identify high K-Score / low K-Score pairs within the same sector for market-neutral relative value investment strategies
  • Pre-earnings screening: Combine high K-Score with upcoming earnings catalyst to identify statistically favorable setups for event-driven investment positioning
  • Risk reduction screening: Use K-Score as an automated quality filter to exclude fundamentally deteriorating positions from an existing portfolio before market stress events

Where Kavout Has Limitations: Use Cases to Avoid

  • Day trading and scalping: K-Score updates daily, not intraday — it provides no edge for sub-daily trading timeframes
  • Macro-driven investing: Factor models can lag dramatically during macro regime shifts; K-Score alone is insufficient for macro-overlay investment strategies
  • International and crypto markets: Coverage is focused on US equities — international investors need complementary tools for non-US market exposure
⚠️ Important Investment Warning: The K-Score is a statistical ranking based on historical factor relationships — not a guarantee of future returns. Past factor performance does not guarantee future outperformance. All investment decisions carry risk of capital loss. Always implement personal risk management through position sizing, stop-losses, and diversification alongside K-Score signals.

4. Recommended Portfolio Workflow with Kavout

Based on our testing, this structured investment workflow extracts the most consistent value from Kavout's toolkit:

💼 Systematic Investment Workflow Using K-Score

1

Universe Definition

Define your investable universe using fundamental filters: Market Cap >$500M, Daily Volume >500K shares, sector preferences. This prevents K-Score signals on illiquid micro-caps.

2

K-Score Filtering

Apply K-Score ≥ 8 filter to your defined universe. This typically narrows a 3,000-stock universe to 150-300 candidates — manageable for further analysis.

3

Sector Diversification Check

Review sector concentration in your high K-Score list. If tech represents 60%+ of candidates, apply sector caps to maintain diversification in your investment approach.

4

Chart & Catalyst Verification

For top 20-30 candidates, manually review price charts for technical setup quality and check upcoming earnings/catalyst calendar using TradingView or similar tools.

5

Position Sizing & Entry

Size positions based on individual conviction and portfolio risk budget — not equally. Higher K-Score combined with stronger technical setup warrants higher allocation.

6

Weekly Score Monitoring

Review K-Score updates weekly. Positions dropping below K-Score 5 warrant review. Scores below 3 should trigger systematic exit consideration regardless of current P&L.

5. Pros & Cons Analysis

✅ Key Strengths

  • ✓ Democratizes quant factor investing: Delivers institutional-style multi-factor model outputs — functionality that previously required a Bloomberg Terminal + proprietary quant team — with quote-based pricing for professional user
  • ✓ Multi-dimensional synthesis: Combines fundamentals, momentum, volatility, and sentiment rather than relying on any single factor — more robust across market regimes
  • ✓ Intuitive 1-9 scoring: Complex quantitative modeling compressed into an accessible format usable by investors without quant backgrounds
  • ✓ Survivorship-bias-free backtesting: Historical K-Score data constructed using point-in-time methodology, avoiding the inflated backtest returns that plague many retail screening tools
  • ✓ Systematic discipline: Rules-based K-Score system removes emotional bias from stock selection and portfolio rebalancing decisions

❌ Limitations

  • ✗ Regime shift vulnerability: Macro shocks (unexpected rate decisions, geopolitical events) can lag factor model adjustments by days — a meaningful risk during high-volatility periods
  • ✗ No direct brokerage execution: Requires manual order execution on third-party platforms — no native integration with Fidelity, TD Ameritrade, or Interactive Brokers
  • ✗ US market only: Limited to US equities and ETFs — international investors need complementary tools for non-US exposure
  • ✗ No intraday signals: Daily score updates unsuitable for short-term trading timeframes below weekly holding periods
  • ✗ Black-box factor weights: Specific model architecture and factor weight assignments are proprietary — analysts cannot audit exact scoring components
